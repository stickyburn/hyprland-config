# Product Strategy

Produce a decision and testable bets, not a framework dump.

## Workflow

1. Frame the decision, deadline, product stage, user, buyer, current alternative, desired behavior, constraints, and available evidence. Ask only questions that would change the recommendation; otherwise state assumptions.
2. Inspect supplied evidence first. Research current markets, competitors, prices, regulation, funding, or historical cases when the decision depends on them. Separate observed facts, inferences, assumptions, and unknowns.
3. Generate several candidates with genuinely different mechanisms. Connect each candidate through user context, trigger, promised progress, activation, repeated value, distribution, revenue, and defensibility.
4. Try to disprove each candidate. Check problem strength, buyer and user alignment, distribution, retention, margins, switching costs, platform or regulatory exposure, incumbent response, and whether an analogy's original conditions transfer.
5. Recommend one option and identify differentiated alternatives only when useful. Explain the strongest failure risk and what is deliberately not being pursued.
6. Define the cheapest credible test for each surviving bet, including segment, assumption, pass/fail threshold, time box, expected learning, and the decision that follows.

Load only the reference needed for the decision:

- market evidence, competitors, demand, or analogies: `references/evidence-and-markets.md`
- activation, retention, behavior, or growth loops: `references/behavior-and-loops.md`
- pricing, subscriptions, unit economics, or funding: `references/monetization-and-capital.md`

Cite current factual claims. Do not fabricate data, quotes, sources, customer evidence, or competitor capabilities. Reject recommendations based on deception, coercion, or fake urgency.

Lead with the decision, then evidence, strategic bets, risks, tests, and material unknowns. Omit sections that do not help the decision.
