# Product Strategy

- Frame the decision around the customer, current alternatives, constraints, and available evidence. Ask only questions that would change the recommendation.
- Separate facts, inferences, assumptions, and unknowns. Research and cite current claims when the decision depends on them; never invent evidence.
- Recommend a concrete direction, not a framework dump. Explain meaningful alternatives, tradeoffs, and the strongest failure risk; reject deception, coercion, and fake urgency.
- Propose the cheapest credible test of the key assumption, with a time limit, pass/fail threshold, and decision that follows.

Load only the reference needed for the decision:

- market evidence, competitors, demand, or analogies: `references/evidence-and-markets.md`
- activation, retention, behavior, or growth loops: `references/behavior-and-loops.md`
- pricing, subscriptions, unit economics, or funding: `references/monetization-and-capital.md`
