# Evidence and Markets

Use for customer discovery, market selection, alternatives, distribution,
and historical analogies. Gather only evidence that can change the decision.

## Evidence Quality

Prefer consequential product behavior and transactions, then direct workflow
observation, interviews about specific past events, and primary sources such
as product docs, pricing, filings, and public data. Transparent research and
independent reporting can supplement them. Reviews/forums reveal vocabulary
and edge cases, not representative prevalence. Vendor content and model memory
are leads, not verification.

Date material sources and link directly. Do not count syndicated copies as
independent corroboration. Preserve contradictions and distinguish fact,
inference, assumption, and unknown. Survey enthusiasm and a popular PMF
threshold are not universal proof; pair them with segment-level behavior.

## Demand and Alternatives

Establish the trigger, frequency, current spend/effort/risk, who feels the
problem, who buys, and who implements. Ask “Walk me through the last time”
and trace actions, costs, attempted alternatives, and why the current approach
survived. Avoid leading pitches and hypothetical willingness-to-pay questions.

Map direct competitors, bundled suites, manual work/internal tools, and doing
nothing. For important alternatives, compare job, segment, value metric,
price, access to customers, switching costs, and strategic advantage. Verify
capabilities in product evidence, not a vendor comparison chart.

Look for a specific opening: an underserved workflow, a costly architectural
constraint, an uneconomic segment, a trusted channel, or a privacy/deployment
requirement incumbents cannot credibly satisfy. A missing feature alone is
not a defensible opportunity.

## Reach and Distribution

Estimate reachable accounts × realistic annual value × attainable share over
the chosen channel/time horizon. State the geography, population, price, and
adoption assumptions. Use top-down TAM only as an outer bound.

Connect the trigger to where people seek help, who already has trusted access,
and a repeatable channel compatible with price and margin. Product use may
create collaboration or an artifact that reaches another qualified user;
do not assume exposure automatically creates demand or a loop.

## Analogies and Synthesis

For an analogy, record the exact product/period, sourced outcome, plausible
mechanism, enabling conditions, transfer limits, and smallest adaptation to
test. Include failed cases and incumbent responses when useful. A founder's
retrospective explains their theory; it does not prove causality.

Synthesize the demand signal, status-quo force, opening, economic constraint,
and fatal unknown. Use a compact evidence table only when comparison helps.
If the unknown cannot be resolved by research, propose a consequential test
or a data request. Do not keep researching to manufacture certainty.
