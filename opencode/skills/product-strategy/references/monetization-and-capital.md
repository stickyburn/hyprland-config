# Monetization and Capital

Use for pricing, packaging, revenue models, unit economics, and business
funding decisions. Tie recommendations to value and evidence.

## Pricing and Packaging

Map user, buyer, approver, budget, valuable outcome, alternative spend,
frequency, and cost/risk to serve. Segment by job and realized value, not
company size alone. Choose a value metric customers can understand and
forecast, that tracks benefit and works with margin and metering. Successful
usage should not become a surprising punishment.

Package meaningful differences in workflow, service, or control. Do not
use bad decoys or hold basic safety, privacy, export, or accessibility hostage.
Prefer actual purchases/renewals, paid pilots, and replacement spend over
stated willingness to pay. Controlled offers and realistic choice/invoice
tests can help; competitor prices require adjustment for segment and scope.
Do not infer precise prices from compliments or a tiny convenience sample.

Anchors and price endings cannot repair missing value. Transparent comparison
and consequential tests matter more than cosmetic pricing psychology.

## Revenue Model Fit

| Model | When it fits |
|---|---|
| Subscription | Recurring or accumulating value at a credible cadence |
| Usage | Consumption tracks value and variable cost |
| Transaction | Product enables an exchange |
| License/one-time | Durable local utility |
| Services | Expertise and labor, honestly priced |
| Outcome/hybrid | Measurable attribution or distinct fixed/variable value |

For subscriptions, inspect activation, cohorts, voluntary/payment churn,
expansion/contraction, margin, and support burden. Cancellation friction is
not a retention strategy. Protect refunds, trust, and regret as guardrails.

## Economics

```text
gross margin = (revenue - variable cost to serve) / revenue
CAC payback months = acquisition cost / monthly gross profit from a new customer
simple subscription LTV ~= ARPA × gross margin / logo churn
```

Use matching periods and state assumptions. The LTV shortcut is unreliable
with small/heterogeneous cohorts, changing churn, expansion, annual contracts,
or usage revenue. Prefer cohort cash flows and contribution margin when available.
Check whether price supports acquisition/service, growth changes margins,
discounts buy actual value, and which assumption changes runway most.

## Capital and Tests

Funding should unlock a measurable milestone. Compare only relevant sources:
customer finance/bootstrapping for early saleable value and control, grants for
eligible work, debt for predictable repayment, angels for early uncertainty,
venture for a plausible outlier-scale capital-accelerated engine, and strategic
capital for specific access with dependency risk.

State amount, timing, runway, contingency, dilution/control or repayment,
cost of waiting, and next options. Distinguish accelerating known demand from
subsidizing an unresolved bet. A profitable niche need not be venture-backed.
Do not invent growth forecasts to make it fundable.

Test the key assumption cheaply: paid pilot for demand, real-volume invoice
for a metric, comparable offers for price, repeat value/cohorts for subscription
fit, and milestone/runway scenarios for financing. Specify duration, proposed
threshold, guardrails, and the next decision rather than collecting opinions.
